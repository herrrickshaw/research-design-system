The headline claim, stated as a finding

One sentence that says what the reader learns and what it rests on — not what the document is about.

In brief
  • Lead with the number that changes a decision.
  • Say what would have to be true for it to be wrong.
  • Name the source and its vintage.

1What the data says

Body copy sits in a serif face at a measured width. Keep paragraphs to one idea. Put the caveat next to the claim, not in a footnote.

Side box — the counter-argument Use this for the case against your own finding. If there isn't one, the finding is probably too weak to publish.
Warning box — what would break this Reserve for a material caveat: a stale vintage, a disputed reporter, an assumption the conclusion cannot survive.

Sources & method. Name every dataset, its vintage, and the retrieval date. Where two reporters disagree, say so and state which you used.
